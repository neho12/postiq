Final identity: 01 Ramp

Four
concepts.

Round 1 (a purple ring with a bead) was rejected as generic, so nothing from it is carried over. These are four unrelated ideas, each with its own symbol, wordmark and logic. Every row below is the same test, so you can compare column by column. Switch the palette at the top: the primary direction (warm off-white, ink, lime) or the alternative (white, black, orange).

01

Ramp

B Typographic experiment

Nine letters, nine different weights and widths. OPEN is dense, heavy and compressed; FIELD relaxes into wide, light, airy forms. The same ramp fills a 3 x 3 square that holds the whole name: OPE / NFI / ELD.

Why it worksThe word opens: weight and width travel from a closed, heavy O to a hairline D, so the logo acts out its own name.

02

Threshold

A Open space

A door in plan view, drawn the way an architect draws it: a wall with an interrupted boundary and the quarter-circle field the door sweeps when it opens.

Why it works"Open" and "field" become one geometric act: the wall is interrupted, and everything the door can reach is filled in lime.

03

Skyline

B Proportion

Lowercase with a tiny x-height under towering f, l and d and one deep p. The silhouette of the word is the logo; a single lime dot sits in the hook of the f.

Why it worksExtreme ascenders turn the name into a horizon you recognise before you read it, and the lime dot is the open sky under the f.

04

Clearing

C Graphic system

A field of parallel stems with one circular clearing: a window where any image, number or word can sit. The lockup repeats the logic in type: OPEN is opened by letter-spacing, FIELD sits dense on its lime plot.

Why it worksA Swiss-style system: the field is the pattern, the clearing is the content, and the wordmark spends space where the name says "open".

01 RampNotes

Strengths

  • A silhouette nobody else has: the letters themselves change.
  • Works with no symbol at all; the 3 x 3 square is a free second asset and a ready-made pattern.
  • Lime appears only inside ink (the O counter, the F tile) where it actually has contrast.

Risks

  • The hairline D needs a minimum size (about 140 px wide).
  • The 3 x 3 square cannot shrink to a favicon, so a separate tile mark is needed.
  • Weight gradients are a known trend; the 3 x 3 and the lime counter are what make this one ours.

02 ThresholdNotes

Strengths

  • The strongest symbol at 16 px and in one colour.
  • A real metaphor behind a very abstract shape.
  • Timeless, architectural, nothing like a tech icon.

Risks

  • The wordmark is plain extended caps; the symbol does all the work.
  • Outside architecture, it can read as an abstract frame rather than a door.

03 SkylineNotes

Strengths

  • The quietest and most premium of the four.
  • Recognisable by outline alone.
  • The "fi" pair is a ready-made monogram.

Risks

  • Built on a familiar sans skeleton: it depends on proportion and the dot to be distinctive.
  • "fi" is not an initial, so the monogram needs explaining.

04 ClearingNotes

Strengths

  • The most extendable system: posters, templates, motion.
  • Colour blocks give it impact; it also works carved in pure black and white.

Risks

  • Stripes plus a dot can read as a barcode.
  • The least unique symbol, and the heaviest tone.

My pick

01 Ramp

It is the only concept where the wordmark itself is the idea, and the only one whose letters are all different from each other. That makes it recognisable by shape alone: in greyscale, at a glance, before anyone reads it.

The same move works at two scales (a ramping wordmark and a ramping 3 x 3 square that holds the whole name), which is what makes it feel designed rather than typed. The refined version is built from this concept: see the final identity.

Pick differently if you want

  • The quietest, most premium look: 03 Skyline.
  • The strongest standalone symbol: 02 Threshold.
  • A campaign system first and a logo second: 04 Clearing.

Type. Outlines come from Archivo (concepts 01, 02, 04) and Roboto Flex (concept 03), both under the SIL Open Font License; every letter in the artwork is converted to a vector path, so no font is needed to render a logo. This page uses a Latin subset of Archivo.

Colour. Lime #C7F464 on paper #F2F0E9 is only about 1.1 : 1, so lime is used inside ink shapes or on dark, never as text or a thin line on paper. Ink on lime is about 14 : 1.
